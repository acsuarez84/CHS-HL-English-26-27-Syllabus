Section 4

Individual Oral Pairings

Every non-literary source in the course, matched to a literary work it genuinely shares a global issue with.

The IO is supported by one extract from a non-literary text and one from a literary work. These pairings are starting points, not prescriptions — a student who finds a better connection should take it, and defend it.

Non-literary source Literary work Shared global issue The connection
Tracy Chapman, Tracy Chapman — “Fast Car” The Hour of the Star Culture, identity and community The narrator of “Fast Car” and Macabéa are the same socially invisible poor woman. Both texts ask who is permitted to tell her story, and what the telling costs her.
Tracy Chapman — “Behind the Wall” Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community Harm overheard through a wall, and harm noticed but unspoken at a party: both turn on a witness who registers everything and does nothing.
Dorothea Lange — Damaged Child, Migrant Mother The Hour of the Star Politics, power and justice Documentary witness and its ethics. Lange composes the poor for a public; Lispector’s narrator admits he is doing exactly that, and cannot stop.
Dorothea Lange — full photo essay Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community A face and a caption against a life compressed into six pages — two miniatures that ask how much of a person an image or an epiphany can hold.
Interstellar (Nolan, 2014) The Woman in the Dunes Science, technology and the environment Dust and sand as agents rather than settings. Both works trap people inside an environment that will not negotiate, then ask whether escape or adaptation is the braver choice.
Inception (Nolan, 2010) The Tunnel Art, creativity and the imagination Two obsessive men who build a reality to hold one woman inside it, and cannot tell their construction from the world.
Shutter Island (Scorsese, 2010) The Tunnel Beliefs, values and education The confession that convicts its teller. Both are narrated by a murderer whose account of events the audience trusts until it collapses.
Arrival (Villeneuve, 2016) Selected Poems (Eliot) Science, technology and the environment Language reshaping the experience of time — Eliot’s circular, layered present beside a linguist who learns to remember forwards.
Edward Hopper — Automat, Morning Sun Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community Automat is Miss Brill in paint: a woman alone in public, composing herself for an audience that is not looking.
Edward Hopper — Nighthawks, Early Sunday Morning The Tunnel Culture, identity and community The city as a set of lit windows nobody can enter. Hopper’s geometry does visually what Castel’s narration does verbally.
Kreml Hair Tonic, “My barber knows women” (1944) Othello Culture, identity and community A man is trusted to report what women want. Iago works the same trick, and Othello believes him over Desdemona.
Ad*Access — “Towards a day less war!” Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community The advertised ideal woman against Mansfield’s women measuring themselves against it and falling short.
W. E. B. Du Bois — Data Portraits (1900) Othello Politics, power and justice Du Bois seizes the tools of representation to describe his own people. Othello never gets that chance — Venice narrates him, and he finally narrates himself only in a suicide speech.
Our World in Data · climate infographics The Woman in the Dunes Science, technology and the environment Sand measured and sand endured: the same crisis rendered as a chart and as a life.
Swift, “A Modest Proposal” (1729) The Hour of the Star Politics, power and justice Both hand the reader a narrator who discusses the poor with appalling composure, and both make that composure the real subject.
Key & Peele, “Obama’s Anger Translator” Othello Culture, identity and community The Black man’s carefully governed public voice, and the rage it costs to maintain it. Othello holds that composure in Act 1 and loses it by Act 3.
Sojourner Truth, “Ain’t I a Woman?” (1851) Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community Whose womanhood counts. Truth names the exclusion directly; Mansfield’s servants and spinsters live inside it without naming it.
Frederick Douglass, “What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?” (1852) Othello Politics, power and justice The outsider addressing, in its own rhetoric, the society that refuses him. Both speakers are magnificent and both are alone in the room.
Martin Luther King Jr., “I Have a Dream” (1963) Othello Politics, power and justice Rhetoric as the instrument of justice rather than manipulation — the deliberate counterweight to Iago’s persuasion.
Malala Yousafzai, UN Youth Assembly (2013) Selected Poems (Eliot) Beliefs, values and education What a society believes is worth passing on. Eliot mourns a tradition emptying out; Malala demands entry to one being withheld.
Greta Thunberg, UN Climate Action Summit (2019) The Woman in the Dunes Science, technology and the environment Complicity and inertia. The villagers shovel sand forever because stopping is unthinkable; Thunberg’s charge is that we are doing the same.
Frank Bellew, “Melican Leportee Man” (1879) Othello Culture, identity and community A white artist ventriloquising a Chinese voice, and a Venetian play ventriloquising a Moor. Both ask who holds the pen when an outsider ‘speaks.’
“The Rich Growing Richer…” (1871) The Hour of the Star Politics, power and justice Inequality drawn as caricature and inequality written as one woman’s unremarkable life.
NASA & NOAA climate sites The Woman in the Dunes Science, technology and the environment Institutional authority about nature, set against a novel in which nature answers to no institution at all.
Humans of New York Selected Short Stories (Mansfield) Culture, identity and community A stranger’s life delivered in a paragraph and a photograph — the modernist epiphany rebuilt for the scroll.
