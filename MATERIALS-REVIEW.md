# Instructional Materials — Florida Compliance Review

**Course:** IB Language & Literature HL, Celebration High School, cohort 2026–2028
**Reviewed:** 4 August 2026
**Status:** Teacher self-review. **Not a legal opinion and not a substitute for district
review.** Final authority rests with the school's certified media specialist and the
district's instructional materials process.

## Statutory criteria applied

Material may be objected to, and must be removed within **5 school days** pending
resolution, if it:

- is pornographic or prohibited under **s. 847.012**, or depicts/describes sexual conduct
  as defined in **s. 847.001(19)**;
- is **not suited to student needs** or their ability to comprehend it;
- is **inappropriate for the grade level and age group** it is used with.

Selection must also satisfy **s. 1006.40(3)(d)**. Instruction touching race is governed by
**s. 1000.05**: historical facts, including racial discrimination, may be taught
objectively and without endorsement; instruction may not compel the view that a student
bears personal responsibility for acts committed in the past by others of the same race.

Sources: [s. 1006.28](https://flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2023/1006.28) ·
[CS/CS/HB 1069 (2023)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/1069)

## Items to log with the media specialist before teaching

**1. Bellew cartoon (1879) — the highest-attention item.**
Historical anti-Chinese caricature. Defensible and valuable when taught as analysis of how
racist propaganda constructs its argument, in the documented context of the 1882 Chinese
Exclusion Act. Recommend: log in advance, prepare a one-paragraph rationale, and be ready
to offer an alternative assignment on request.

**2. Hopper, *Morning Sun* (1952).**
Depicts a seated woman in a shift. Not sexual conduct under s. 847.001(19), and it hangs in
a public museum's permanent collection — but figure studies do draw objections. Low risk,
worth noting in advance. *Automat* or *Room in New York* substitute cleanly if preferred.

**3. Tracy Chapman, "Behind the Wall."**
Unaccompanied vocal about domestic violence overheard through a wall. No profanity, no
sexual content. Age-appropriate for grades 11–12; worth naming in the syllabus letter home
so families are not surprised.

**4. Swift, "A Modest Proposal" (1729).**
Canonical satire whose surface proposal is cannibalism. Occasionally challenged by readers
who miss the persona — which is precisely the skill being taught. Low risk; the lesson plan
should make the ironic frame explicit from the outset.

**5. *Interstellar* (PG-13).**
Parent consent for mature titles is already required by the syllabus. Keep the consent form
on file.
