Music — album lyrics

Year 1 · Q1 · 3 weeksCommunication • Culture • Representation • Perspective

Culture, identity and community — how popular music shapes and reflects cultural identity, values, and emotion.

Studied alongside The Tunnel. Chapman’s debut is spare, narrative, and profanity-free: four songs give students first-person voices from inside poverty, which is exactly the terrain Lispector will cover from the outside when The Hour of the Star arrives in Year 2.

  • □Tracy Chapman · Tracy Chapman · Elektra, 1988In copyright — opens the source

    Tracy Chapman, Tracy Chapman (1988) — full albumadded

    “Fast Car” — a narrator who plans her escape in the second person. “Behind the Wall” — unaccompanied voice; domestic violence heard through a wall and never intervened in. “Talkin’ Bout a Revolution” — the rhetoric of coming change. “Mountains o’ Things” — consumer desire voiced by someone who has nothing.

    APAChapman, T. (1988). Tracy Chapman [Album]. Elektra Records.
    MLAChapman, Tracy. Tracy Chapman. Elektra Records, 1988.

Two notes. Your draft assigned this row Art, creativity and the imagination; I have moved it to Culture, identity and community, which is what these lyrics actually do and what makes the Hour of the Star connection work. If you would rather keep the original field, use Hamilton (Miranda) instead — transformation and intertextuality, pairing with Othello. Bolder options, both with explicit content to screen first: To Pimp a Butterfly, Lemonade.

Photography — Dorothea Lange

Year 1 · Q2 · 3 weeksRepresentation • Perspective • Communication

Politics, power and justice — the Great Depression, migrant labour, and the ethics of documentary witness.

Twelve photographs. Most are Farm Security Administration negatives held by the Library of Congress and are public domain, so they are embedded here in full. About the photographer Dorothea Lange · MoMA Studied alongside Eliot’s poems — two bodies of work made in the same decades, one looking outward at a country in crisis, one inward at a self coming apart.

  • White Angel Bread Line, San Francisco, 1933

    A single man turned away from the crowd, arms folded over an empty tin cup. Composition isolates one figure inside a mass. Predates the FSA, so it is not in the public-domain set — view it at MoMA.

    APALange, D. (1933–1938). Photographs [Photographs]. The Museum of Modern Art. https://www.moma.org/artists/3373
    MLALange, Dorothea. Photographs. 1933–1938, Museum of Modern Art, moma.org/artists/3373.

  • The Migrant Mother sequence — frame 1 of 4added

    Nipomo, California, March 1936. Lange shot six exposures in about ten minutes. This is the widest: you can see the tent, the pole, the lantern, the trunk, the empty pie tin, and a second camp in the distance. The context she would later remove.

  • Migrant Mother — frame 2 of 4added

    Closer. The tent pole still cuts the frame and the lantern still sits in shot, but the distance is gone. Ask students what has been given up and what has been gained.

  • Migrant Mother — frame 3 of 4added

    Closer still, and the children begin to turn away from the lens. Lange is removing everything that would let you place this family in a particular camp on a particular day.

  • Migrant Mother — the published frame

    The most reproduced photograph of the era. Set it against the three frames above and the lesson makes itself: the tent, the lantern, the pan, the camp and the children’s faces are all gone. What remains is a face that could belong to any mother anywhere — which is exactly why it worked as an argument, and exactly what it cost the real Florence Owens Thompson, who was identified decades later and said she never saw a cent.

    APALange, D. (1936). Destitute pea pickers in California. Mother of seven children. Age thirty-two. Nipomo, California [Photograph]. Library of Congress. https://www.loc.gov/item/2017762891/
    MLALange, Dorothea. Destitute Pea Pickers in California. Mother of Seven Children. Age Thirty-Two. Nipomo, California. 1936. Library of Congress, loc.gov/item/2017762891/.

  • Damaged Child, Shacktown, Elm Grove, Oklahoma, 1936

    The title editorialises before the viewer looks. A test case for how captions direct reading. Held by MoMA.

  • Plantation Overseer and his Field Hands, Mississippi Delta, June 1936

    Foot on the bumper, men arranged behind. Power staged as a casual pose.

    APALange, D. (1936). Plantation overseer. Mississippi Delta, near Clarksdale, Mississippi [Photograph]. Library of Congress.
    MLALange, Dorothea. Plantation Overseer. Mississippi Delta, near Clarksdale, Mississippi. 1936. Library of Congress.

  • Toward Los Angeles, California, March 1937

    Two men walking away from the lens beneath a billboard promising comfortable travel. Irony built from juxtaposition.

    APALange, D. (1937). Toward Los Angeles, California [Photograph]. Library of Congress.
    MLALange, Dorothea. Toward Los Angeles, California. 1937. Library of Congress.

  • Dust Bowl Farmer with Tractor and Son, near Claud, New Mexico, 1938added

    Machinery and a child in the same frame, on land that is failing. Useful beside Interstellar: the same argument about technology and exhausted ground, forty years before the film.

  • Cotton Picker near Firebaugh, Californiaadded

    A portrait made at working height, not looking down. Compare the camera position here with the overseer photograph.

  • Mother and Three Children in a California Squatter Campadded

    A second mother-and-children composition. Set it beside the Migrant Mother sequence and ask why this one did not become an icon.

  • Once a Missouri Farmer, Now a Migratory Farm Labourer on the Pacific Coastadded

    The caption does the whole argument in one sentence — a before and an after with no image of the before. A model for how a title can carry a thesis.

  • Ex-Tenant Farmer on Relief Grant, Imperial Valley, Californiaadded

    Another caption that names an economic fall. Track how often Lange’s titles specify what someone used to be.

  • Georgia Road Signadded

    Almost no people. Lange photographs a sign and lets the language in the landscape make the point — a bridge from photography into the advertising unit in Year 2.

  • Funeral Cortege, End of an Era in a Small Valley Town, California, 1938

    Lange’s own title names the theme — a study in how a photographer argues before you have looked at anything. Not embedded here: unlike the twelve above, this one is not in the Library of Congress public-domain set. It survives as museum prints, and the museums hold rights to their reproductions — Cleveland lists it as copyrighted with no open-access file. View it at SFMOMA, the Getty, Cleveland or MoMA — or project it from one of those pages in class, which is ordinary classroom use.

Film

Year 1 · Q2 · 1–2 weeksRepresentation • Creativity • Transformation • Perspective

Science, technology and the environment — the environment as an agent that shapes and constrains human life.

Studied in the same quarter as the Lange photographs and The Hour of the Star, and immediately before The Woman in the Dunes in Q3 — the pairing the unit is built around.

  • □Interstellar · Nolan · 2014In copyright — opens the source

    Interstellar (Nolan, 2014) — PG-13added

    Science, technology and the environment. Concepts: Representation, Transformation, Perspective. Blight, dust, and a planet that has stopped cooperating. Set it beside The Woman in the Dunes: both trap people inside an environment that will not negotiate, then ask whether escape or adaptation is the braver choice. Useful scenes for close analysis — the dust storm, the docking sequence, and the time-dilation scene on Miller's planet, where the cost of a decision is measured in years of someone else's life.

    APANolan, C. (Director). (2014). Interstellar [Film]. Paramount Pictures; Warner Bros. Pictures.
    MLAInterstellar. Directed by Christopher Nolan, Paramount Pictures, 2014.

Art — Edward Hopper

Year 1 · Q3 · 2–3 weeksRepresentation • Perspective • Culture

Culture, identity and community — urban isolation and the American mid-century imagination.

Your draft named Hopper but no paintings. Six are proposed below, matching the six Lange photographs so the two units can be compared directly: documentary witness against composed solitude. About the painter Edward Hopper · Whitney Museum Why these are links, not pictures. The Lange photographs above are embedded because she made them as a federal employee, so they are public domain. Hopper is the opposite case: he died in 1967, his estate rights are actively managed, and the museums mark his work accordingly — the Art Institute lists Nighthawks as not public domain, and every Hopper the Metropolitan holds is flagged the same way, including canvases from the 1920s. Open each painting at its museum and project it from there, which is ordinary classroom use.

  • Nighthawks, 1942added

    Four people, no visible door. The most famous painting of loneliness in American art.

    MLAHopper, Edward. Nighthawks. 1942, Art Institute of Chicago, artic.edu/artworks/111628/nighthawks.

  • Automat, 1927added

    A woman alone with a coffee cup and a black window. This is Miss Brill. Link is the Des Moines Art Center’s own teaching sheet, with discussion prompts.

Advertisements

Year 2 · Q1 · 2 weeksCommunication • Representation • Culture • Perspective

Culture, identity and community — contemporary brand identity and historical persuasion, across a century.

Contemporary campaigns paired with the Duke Ad*Access and Emergence of Advertising archives, so students see the same persuasive moves a century apart.

  • Nike — “Purpose, powered by athletes”

    Identity / Representation. Politics, power and justice. Nike frames race, gender, and the athlete-as-outsider.

    APANike. (n.d.). Purpose, powered by athletes. Retrieved August 4, 2026, from https://www.nike.com/gb/purpose
    MLA“Purpose, Powered by Athletes.” Nike, nike.com/gb/purpose. Accessed 4 Aug. 2026.

  • The Dove Self-Esteem Project

    Identity / Perspective. Culture, identity and community. Self-image against how the world sees you.

  • Patagonia — Environmental Activism

    Communication / Perspective. Science, technology and the environment. The anti-consumption paradox; humans against, and inside, nature.

  • Ben & Jerry’s — Activism

    Communication / Culture. Politics, power and justice. Corporate activism as brand identity. Select specific campaigns rather than assigning the whole site — the page spans many advocacy positions, and a defined extract is both easier to teach and easier to defend at review.

  • LEGO — About Us

    Creativity / Representation. Art, creativity and the imagination. Play, imagination, and how a brand sells childhood itself.

  • “Towards a day less war!” — Ad*Access

    Representation. Culture, identity and community. 1911–1955 advertising constructing the “ideal woman.”

    APATowards a day less war! [Advertisement]. (n.d.). Ad*Access, Duke University Libraries. https://repository.duke.edu/dc/adaccess/BH1901
    MLA“Towards a Day Less War!” Advertisement. Ad*Access, Duke U Libraries, repository.duke.edu/dc/adaccess/BH1901.

  • “My barber knows women” — Ad*Accessadded

    Kreml Hair Tonic, 1944, published in Cosmopolitan. It sells hair tonic and war bonds in the same breath. Concepts: Representation, Perspective, Communication. Culture, identity and community — a man is trusted to report what women want; secondary Politics, power and justice — wartime propaganda fused with product copy. This ad is the hinge between the two either side of it: it does the ideal-woman work of “Towards a day less war!” and the state-persuasion work of “Home…” simultaneously.

    APAKreml Hair Tonic. (1944). My barber knows women [Advertisement]. Cosmopolitan. Ad*Access, Duke University Libraries. https://repository.duke.edu/dc/adaccess/BH0543
    MLA“My Barber Knows Women.” Advertisement for Kreml Hair Tonic. Cosmopolitan, 1944. Ad*Access, Duke U Libraries, repository.duke.edu/dc/adaccess/BH0543.

  • “Home…” — Ad*Access

    Communication / Perspective. Politics, power and justice. State persuasion engineering fear and loyalty.

  • The World’s Industrial Exhibition of 1853 — Season Ticket

    Culture / Representation. Culture, identity and community. The birth of the manufactured consumer self.

Infographics

Year 2 · Q1 · 2 weeksCommunication • Representation • Creativity

Science, technology and the environment — data visualisation, the rhetorical use of statistics, and scientific literacy.

Your draft named the text type but no examples. The set below deliberately spans 1858 to the present, and splits across two fields of inquiry so the unit is not only about climate.

  • W. E. B. Du Bois — Data Portraits, 1900 Paris Expositionadded

    Politics, power and justice. Hand-drawn, hand-coloured statistical charts on Black American life, made to argue against a world’s fair that had excluded that argument. Public domain at the Library of Congress. The single richest text in this unit: it is data visualisation as counter-representation, by the people being represented.

    APADu Bois, W. E. B. (1900). The Georgia Negro: A social study [Data visualizations]. Library of Congress. https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/anedub/
    MLADu Bois, W. E. B. The Georgia Negro: A Social Study. 1900. Library of Congress, loc.gov/pictures/collection/anedub/.

  • Du Bois — Negro population of Georgia and Family budgetsadded

    Two more plates from the same set. The budget charts turn household spending into a stacked bar a century before the form was standard; the population map colours a state by density. Show these beside Our World in Data and ask what has actually changed.

  • Our World in Data — CO₂ and greenhouse gas emissionsadded

    Science, technology and the environment. CC-licensed, endlessly re-chartable. Good for asking how axis choices and baselines change the argument.

    APARitchie, H., Rosado, P., & Roser, M. (n.d.). CO₂ and greenhouse gas emissions. Our World in Data. Retrieved August 4, 2026, from https://ourworldindata.org/co2-and-greenhouse-gas-emissions
    MLARitchie, Hannah, et al. “CO₂ and Greenhouse Gas Emissions.” Our World in Data, ourworldindata.org/co2-and-greenhouse-gas-emissions. Accessed 4 Aug. 2026.

  • Bloomberg — “What’s Really Warming the World?” (2015)added

    Science, technology and the environment. Scrolling narrative visualisation that eliminates competing explanations one at a time — argument structure made visible.

  • Florence Nightingale — the rose diagram, 1858added

    Politics, power and justice. Optional. The origin point of persuasive data design, invented to force a government to act.

Satire & Humour

Year 2 · Q1Communication • Creativity • Perspective

Politics, power and justice — satire as social and political critique; the rhetoric of irony.

Studied in the same quarter as The Hour of the Star. Swift anchors the unit; Key & Peele bring it into living memory and connect it straight back to Othello in Q1.

  • Jonathan Swift, “A Modest Proposal” (1729)added

    Free full text. The permanent test case for irony: students who miss the persona miss everything. Pairs with The Hour of the Star — both hand you a narrator who discusses the poor with appalling composure, and both make that composure the subject.

    APASwift, J. (1729). A modest proposal. Project Gutenberg. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1080
    MLASwift, Jonathan. A Modest Proposal. 1729. Project Gutenberg, gutenberg.org/ebooks/1080.

  • Key & Peele, “Obama’s Anger Translator”added

    Code-switching performed as a two-man sketch: the governed public voice and the rage it costs to hold. Pairs with Othello, who keeps that composure in Act 1 and loses it by Act 3. Linked to the official Comedy Central page rather than a YouTube upload, because the widely-circulated YouTube version is the uncensored cut. Preview before showing.

    APAKey, K.-M., & Peele, J. (2012). Obama’s anger translator — meet Luther [TV series episode]. In Key & Peele. Comedy Central.
    MLA“Obama’s Anger Translator — Meet Luther.” Key & Peele, created by Keegan-Michael Key and Jordan Peele, Comedy Central, 2012.

  • Mark Twain, “Advice to Youth” (1882)added

    Short, public domain, and gentler than Swift — a good first exercise in spotting a persona before tackling the harder text.

    APATwain, M. (1882). Advice to youth. Project Gutenberg. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3007
    MLATwain, Mark. “Advice to Youth.” 1882. Project Gutenberg, gutenberg.org/ebooks/3007.

  • Babylon Bee / Onion-style headlines — teacher-selected setadded

    Headline-level satire is excellent for quick starters on register and format parody, but no satirical news site is uniformly classroom-safe. Build a short set of individually vetted headlines and distribute those, rather than sending students to a live site.

On Swift. “A Modest Proposal” is an essay being used here as a non-literary text type (the satirical pamphlet). That is standard practice in Language and Literature, and since it is not one of your six works it creates no IB conflict.

Political Cartoons

Year 2 · Q2Representation • Communication • Perspective

Politics, power and justice — visual rhetoric, caricature, and ideological framing.

Your three cartoons were listed without annotation. Each is identified and mapped below.

  • Frank Bellew, “Melican Leportee Man Want Gabbee…” — Harper’s Weekly, 14 June 1879, p. 476added

    Culture, identity and community / Politics, power and justice. Concepts: Representation, Perspective. One of a Bellew series presenting American society through a fabricated “Chinese point of view,” mimicking Chinese calligraphy in the drawing and an exaggerated accent in the text — three years before the Chinese Exclusion Act. Pairs with Othello: who holds the pen when an outsider is made to ‘speak.’ Classroom framing. This is historical racist caricature. Florida law permits teaching historical facts including racial discrimination, taught objectively and without endorsement (s. 1000.05); it does not permit instruction that any student bears personal responsibility for actions committed in the past by others of the same race. Frame the lesson as analysis of how the artifact manufactures its argument — technique, audience, and the Exclusion Act it preceded. Log it with your media specialist before teaching.

    APABellew, F. (1879, June 14). Melican leportee man want gabbee [Cartoon]. Harper’s Weekly, 476.
    MLABellew, Frank. “Melican Leportee Man Want Gabbee.” Harper’s Weekly, 14 June 1879, p. 476.

  • “The Rich Growing Richer, the Poor Growing Poorer” — Harper’s Weekly, 2 September 1871added

    Politics, power and justice. Concepts: Representation, Communication. Gilded Age inequality at the height of the Tweed Ring. Pairs with The Hour of the Star and the Lange photographs.

    APAThe rich growing richer, the poor growing poorer [Cartoon]. (1871, September 2). Harper’s Weekly.
